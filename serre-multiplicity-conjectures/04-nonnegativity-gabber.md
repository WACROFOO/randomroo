# 4. Nonnegativity (M2): Gabber's theorem (c. 1995)

**Theorem (Gabber).** Let $R$ be a regular local ring and $M, N$ finitely
generated $R$-modules with $\ell(M \otimes_R N) < \infty$. Then
$\chi(M, N) \ge 0$.

Ofer Gabber proved this around 1995. Famously, he never published it; the
standard written accounts are:

- P. Berthelot, *Altérations de variétés algébriques (d'après A. J. de Jong)*,
  Séminaire Bourbaki, Exposé 815 (1995–96) — includes an appendix on Gabber's
  argument;
- P. Roberts, *Recent developments on Serre's multiplicity conjectures:
  Gabber's proof of the nonnegativity conjecture*, L'Enseign. Math. **44**
  (1998), 305–324;
- M. Hochster's lecture notes on intersection multiplicities.

## 4.1 The new input: de Jong's alterations

The proof became possible only after A. J. de Jong's 1996 theorem
(*Smoothness, semi-stability and alterations*, Publ. Math. IHÉS **83**):

> For any integral Noetherian scheme $X$ that is excellent of finite
> dimension (e.g. of finite type over a complete local ring), there exists an
> **alteration** $\pi : X' \to X$ — a proper, surjective, generically finite
> morphism — with $X'$ **regular**.

An alteration is weaker than a resolution of singularities (it allows a finite
extension of function fields), but it exists in all characteristics, including
mixed characteristic — exactly where resolution is unknown and where M2 was
open.

## 4.2 Reductions

By the standard dévissage (§1.1) assume $M = R/P$, $N = R/Q$, $R$ complete,
and — by Serre's results (§2) — that $R$ is ramified of mixed characteristic.
Two further classical reductions (both in Roberts' exposition):

1. By the vanishing theorem (M3, already proven) and additivity, one may
   assume $\dim R/P + \dim R/Q = \dim R$, the critical case; nonnegativity in
   lower dimension is just M3.
2. One may replace $R/Q$ by a suitable complete intersection cut out inside it:
   choosing a system of parameters $\underline y$ of $R/Q$ and using
   associativity-type formulas, $\chi(R/P, R/Q)$ is expressed through
   $\chi\big(R/P,\, R/(\underline y')\big)$-type terms, reducing M2 to the case
   where the second module is $R/J$ with $J$ generated by a system of
   parameters-style sequence. Equivalently, using Koszul complexes,
   nonnegativity reduces to a statement about the sign of an Euler
   characteristic against a Koszul complex twisted onto
   $X = \operatorname{Spec}(R/P)$.

After these steps the statement takes a geometric form: on the (typically
singular) scheme $X = \operatorname{Spec}(R/P)$ of dimension $d$, one must
show a certain intersection-theoretic degree, computed from $d$
"hypersurface-type" classes meeting $X$ properly at the closed point, is
nonnegative.

## 4.3 The mechanism

Apply de Jong: choose a regular alteration $\pi : X' \to X$ of degree
$\deg \pi = m$. Properness lets one compare intersection numbers upstairs and
downstairs by the projection formula: up to the positive factor $m$, the
quantity to control becomes an honest intersection number **on the regular
scheme $X'$** — where one now argues geometrically.

Concretely, Gabber blows up the pullback of the maximal ideal so that it
becomes an invertible ideal; on the resulting projective birational
modification $X'' \to X'$ (still regular after a further alteration if
needed), the relevant class is the first Chern class of the line bundle
$\mathcal O(1)$ of the blowup, which is **relatively ample**, hence its top
self-intersection against the exceptional fibre is a degree of a line bundle
with semipositivity properties. The final computation exhibits
$m \cdot \chi(R/P, R/Q)$ (in the reduced form of §4.2) as

$$\int c_1(\mathcal O(1))^{\,d} \cdot (\text{effective cycle}) \;\ge\; 0,$$

a degree of a nef-type class on projective fibres — manifestly nonnegative.
Dividing by $m > 0$ gives $\chi \ge 0$. ∎ (sketch)

The full argument requires care with: passing between $\chi$ and cycle-level
intersection numbers via the singular Riemann–Roch of Fulton (or Roberts'
local Chern characters); controlling the difference terms coming from the
generically finite (not birational) nature of $\pi$; and $p$-torsion issues
special to mixed characteristic. Roberts (1998) gives a complete account in
~20 pages.

## 4.4 Why this does not give positivity

The proof shows the intersection degree is $\ge 0$ because it is a degree of a
semipositive (nef) class — but nef classes can have degree $0$ on special
cycles. The alteration destroys the tight bookkeeping that would be needed to
show the degree is **strictly** positive exactly when
$\dim R/P + \dim R/Q = \dim R$: the correction terms from
$\deg \pi > 1$ and from the blowup can a priori eat the strict inequality.
Sharpening this to $> 0$ is precisely the open positivity conjecture (§5).
